How Ideas Become Real

From something in your head to something you can act on.

A six-week practical programme in Dunoon for local people who want to move something forward. You bring what you are working on. Together we make sense of it, decide what matters now, and take a real step every week.

Save my place

Six weekly sessions · Two hours each · Small group of 6–8 · Donation-based · Starts end of August

This might be you

You may have an idea you have been thinking about for months, but do not know how to begin.

You may run a local business and know something needs to change, but cannot work out what to tackle first.

You might be trying to build a website, develop a service, organise a community project, reach new customers, or test whether an idea could work.

You do not need a business plan, technical experience or a fully formed idea. You bring what you are working on, and we help you make sense of it.

And this is not a course about AI or digital technology. We will use useful tools where they help, but the focus is your business, project or idea.

The six weeks

One wee step at a time.

Everyone works on their own thing, so no two people leave with the same result. Here is the shape of it.

  • 1
    What are you trying to move forward?Leave with a clear focus, a realistic goal and your first wee step.
  • 2
    Who is it for, and what do they need?Leave with a clearer sense of who it is for, and useful questions to ask them.
  • 3
    What is the smallest useful version?Leave with a small, affordable test and a clear idea of what to learn from it.
  • 4
    Make something usefulLeave with something real you can use, share or test.
  • 5
    Take it into the real worldLeave with a real action taken, and information you did not have before.
  • 6
    Decide what happens nextLeave with your next three actions, a 30-day direction and people to stay connected with.

About Laura

I’m Laura, the person behind Hideaway.

For the last 20 years, I’ve worked across customer engagement, events, product development, community building and corporate partnerships. Most of that work has come down to people: getting them into a room, helping them make sense of complicated things, and turning loose ideas into something useful.

At some point, I realised I was less interested in being the person who built everything for everyone else. What I care about is helping people work out what they are trying to do, make a decision and get moving. That is where Hideaway came from.

I live here in Dunoon, and I meet plenty of capable, creative people with businesses, projects and ideas they want to develop. The problem is rarely a lack of ambition. More often, they have too many things competing for attention, do not know where to begin, or have convinced themselves they need to have everything worked out first. Rather than another course, or someone arriving with all the answers, what usually helps is a bit of space, some structure, useful questions and the right tools for the job.

That is what these sessions are designed to offer. Rather than telling people what their idea should become, my role is to help them get clearer, make the work manageable and take a practical next step.

I am building things of my own too, so none of this comes from a safe distance. I know what it is like to have too many ideas, spend too long planning, change your mind, question the whole thing and eventually learn by doing it.

Hideaway is a place to work through that properly, with other people, and get something moving.

The one rule

Come with something real you want to move forward, so you leave with something changed rather than just a nice blether.
